## PROJECT OVERVIEW

Many students need a comfortable, quiet, and ergonomically supportive place within the campus where they can read, review lessons, complete assignments, and work collaboratively with peers. However, current school environments frequently subject students to high ambient noise, crowded corridors, rigid seating, and spaces unsuited for prolonged cognitive concentration.

The **StudySpace Project** aims to create a simple, modular, accessible, and student-centered study corner inside the school campus. Adhering to the principles of **Human-Centered Design (HCD)** and the **Design Thinking** framework (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test), the project prioritizes direct inquiry into user experiences over pre-emptive assumptions. 

By engaging students, teachers, and school operations personnel, the project integrates physical spatial planning, acoustic damping, modular ergonomics, and behavioral guidelines into an actionable, sustainable micro-environment that fosters deep focus and peer collaboration.

# CHAPTER 1: UNDERSTANDING THE PROBLEM

### 1.1 Background of the Problem
Secondary school students spend upwards of 30 to 40 hours per week engaged in formal schooling, with additional hours dedicated to independent study, exam preparation, and performance task completion. The quality of cognitive tasks—such as analytical reading, memory consolidation, and collaborative problem-solving—is directly influenced by physical environmental parameters including noise levels, spatial density, lighting, and posture ergonomics.

Preliminary campus walk-through observations revealed that when the library reached capacity, students were forced to sit on concrete hallway benches, cafeteria dining tables, and stairwell landings. These areas suffer from continuous foot traffic, echoes, poor lumbar support, and absence of electrical outlets. Consequently, students experience cognitive fatigue, distractibility, and elevated academic stress.

### 1.2 Area of Concern
**School Physical Learning Environment:** Usability, acoustic zoning, and accessibility of passive study zones within existing campus architecture.

### 1.3 Initial Problem Statement
> *"Students lack a dedicated, comfortable, quiet, and easily accessible space within the school premises for focused individual study and small-group academic collaboration."*

### 1.4 Users and Stakeholders

#### Primary Users
- **Senior High School Students (Grades 11 & 12):** Require dedicated environments for research writing, STEM problem sets, and college entrance review.
- **Junior High School Students:** Require accessible waiting and study areas between class periods.

#### Secondary Users
- **Faculty & Subject Teachers:** Benefit from students submitting higher-quality outputs and utilizing consultation micro-corners.
- **Peer Tutors & Academic Clubs:** Need flexible group tables for review sessions.

#### Stakeholder Matrix

| Stakeholder Group | Primary Interest | Operational Constraints & Concerns |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Students** | Quietness, ergonomic comfort, device charging, WiFi connectivity | Risk of space monopolization by social groups |
| **Faculty** | Enhanced academic focus, consultation space | Supervision visibility, student noise management |
| **School Administration** | Space utilization efficiency, campus aesthetics | Budget allocation, building code and fire safety compliance |
| **Facilities & Maintenance** | Ease of cleaning, furniture durability | Material wear, electrical load safety, trash buildup |
| **Guidance / Wellness Office**| Stress reduction, inclusive student climate | Safe, welcoming environment free from exclusionary behavior |

# CHAPTER 2: LISTENING TO USERS AND UNDERSTANDING CONTEXTS

The team conducted empirical field inquiry involving $N = 35$ students across multiple academic strands and grade levels, complemented by observations and faculty interviews.

### 2.1 Inquiry Methods
1. **Paper and Digital Student Survey ($N = 35$):** Gauging frequency of campus study, top distractions, and prioritized amenities.
2. **Informal *Kuwentuhan* (Semi-Structured Focus Interviews, $n = 12$):** Eliciting honest emotional accounts of exam preparation difficulties on campus.
3. **Passive Spatial Observation (20 observation sessions over 2 weeks):** Logging foot traffic density, decibel fluctuations, and hallway sitting postures.
4. **Key Informant Interview ($n = 2$):** Interview with the school librarian and physical plant custodian regarding facility limitations.

### 2.2 Survey Instrument Sample Questions
1. *Where do you typically study while waiting between classes or after dismissal?*
2. *What is the primary factor that disrupts your study concentration on campus?* (Noise / Lack of seats / Lighting / Lack of charging / Other)
3. *On average, how many hours per week do you need a dedicated quiet space on campus?*
4. *What study mode do you utilize most frequently?* (Solo deep reading / Pair review / Group discussion)
5. *Which furniture feature is most critical for your comfort?* (Padded seat / Wide desk space / Lumbar support / Footrest)
6. *Would you abide by a student-monitored "Whisper Policy" in a dedicated corner?* (Yes / No / Uncertain)

### 2.3 Qualitative User Voice Summaries
- *"Masyadong maingay sa canteen at hallway; kahit naka-earphones, rinig pa rin ang sigawan kapag break time."* (G12 ASSH Student)
- *"Yung tables sa library laging punuan bago mag-midterms. Napipilitan kaming mag-floor sitting sa hagdan."* (G11 STEM Student)
- *"Sana may separate area yung nagdi-discuss ng group project sa mga nagbabasa nang tahimik. Nagkakahiyaan kapag magkasama."* (G12 ABM Student)
- *"Wala kaming masaksakan ng laptop kapag low-batt na habang gumagawa ng research defense presentation."* (G12 TVL Student)
- *"Ayoko nung upuan na matigas na kahoy; after 30 minutes masakit na sa likod at balikat."* (G11 HUMSS Student)

### 2.4 Field Observation Findings
- **Hallway Decibel Spikes:** Ambient noise in standard corridors during class interchanges averaged $68\text{--}78\text{ dB}$, far exceeding the recommended $40\text{--}45\text{ dB}$ for reading comprehension.
- **Postural Strain:** 82% of students observed studying in corridors displayed forward-head slouching and lack of lumbar support on non-ergonomic benches.
- **Space Contention:** Groups of 4–5 students frequently occupied large library tables intended for 8 solo readers, generating interpersonal friction.

### 2.5 Ethical Considerations & Research Protocols
- **Voluntary Participation:** All students were informed that participation had no bearing on academic grades.
- **Informed Consent & Anonymity:** Surveys did not require full student names or ID numbers; qualitative quotes were anonymized.
- **Photographic Consent:** Explicit permissions were secured prior to capturing documentation photographs of study locations, ensuring no identifying facial details were disseminated.

# CHAPTER 4: CO-DESIGN AND IDEATION

### 4.1 Concept Divergence
The design team brainstormed six candidate concepts:
1. **Concept 1 (Fixed Quiet Study Corner):** Permanent wall-mounted cubicles inside a dead-end corridor.
2. **Concept 2 (Portable Acoustic Study Booth):** Heavy standalone enclosed plywood acoustic telephone-style booth.
3. **Concept 3 (Outdoor Eco-Garden Study Gazebo):** Shaded outdoor garden tables with solar lighting.
4. **Concept 4 (Library Micro-Dividers):** Adding cardboard partitions onto existing library tables.
5. **Concept 5 (Modular StudySpace Station):** Reconfigurable geometric desks with movable acoustic fabric screens and dual-zone floor planning.
6. **Concept 6 (Digital Booking Study Pod):** Smart pod with RFID access card reader and automated power.

### 4.2 SCAMPER Activity Matrix

| Strategy | SCAMPER Application to Candidate Concepts |
| :--- | :--- |
| **S**ubstitute | Substitute costly commercial acoustic foam with recycled egg-cartons wrapped in fire-retardant burlap/linen fabric. |
| **C**ombine | Combine individual writing cubby tables with nesting capability so four solo desks lock into one circular collaboration table. |
| **A**dapt | Adapt Japanese micro-apartment modularity to fit a standard $3.5\text{ m} \times 4.0\text{ m}$ school lobby nook. |
| **M**odify / Magnify | Modify desktop edges with raised non-roll lip and built-in mobile phone / tablet docking slots. |
| **P**ut to Another Use | Use movable acoustic screens as dry-erase whiteboard brainstorm surfaces on one side and bulletin felt on the other. |
| **E**liminate | Eliminate fixed privacy booths that obstruct natural light, airflow, and faculty supervision lines of sight. |
| **R**earrange | Rearrange table orientation so solo readers face wall murals and greenery, while group pods face inward. |

### 4.3 Idea Selection Criteria
- **Context Fit (Weight: 20%):** Conforms to school floor space, safety corridors, and aesthetic guidelines.
- **Feasibility & Cost (Weight: 20%):** Can be realized using readily accessible campus tools and low capital budget.
- **Ergonomics & Acoustic Impact (Weight: 20%):** Measurable comfort improvements and decibel reduction.
- **Flexibility & Modularity (Weight: 15%):** Transitions quickly between individual focus and 4-person collaborative tasks.
- **Accessibility & Safety (Weight: 15%):** Complies with wheelchair clearances and clear fire exit egress.
- **Environmental Sustainability (Weight: 10%):** Utilizes durable, recyclable, or upcycled materials.

### 4.5 Selected Solution: "StudySpace: The Modular Dual-Zone Corner"
The team selected **Concept 5**. The final solution incorporates:
- **Zoned Spatial Layout:** Clear differentiation between "Focus Solo Alcove" (silent) and "Collaborative Pod" (whisper-level).
- **Trapezoidal Reconfigurable Tables:** Enables standalone single-occupant orientation or polygon grouping for 3–5 students.
- **Dual-Sided Acoustic Partition Baffles:** Sound-absorbing recycled core wrapped in canvas, doubling as visual privacy screens.
- **Safe Cable-Managed Power Troughs:** Centralized overhead/edge power strip with circuit breaker protection to prevent tripping.
- **Student Social Compact Signage:** Visual behavioral icons setting normative noise expectations without aggressive policing.

### 5.1 Prototype Description
The team constructed a 1:10 physical scale spatial model paired with full-scale 1:1 cardboard mockups of the desktop geometries and partition screens to validate anthropometric clearances.

### 5.2 Testable Hypotheses & Assumptions
1. *Hypothesis 1 (Zoning):* Visual divider screens reduce visual distraction frequency by at least 50% compared to open tables.
2. *Hypothesis 2 (Modularity):* Students can rearrange table configurations independently in under 60 seconds without tools.
3. *Hypothesis 3 (Acoustic Attenuation):* Padded fabric baffles reduce conversational flutter echoes, lowering ambient noise by $4\text{--}7\text{ dB}$.
4. *Hypothesis 4 (Clearance):* A minimum walkway clearance of $900\text{ mm}$ preserves accessibility and fire exit requirements.

# CHAPTER 6: USER TESTING AND FEEDBACK

### 6.1 Testing Participants
- **10 Senior High School Students** (5 Male, 5 Female; across Grade 11 & 12 strands).
- **2 Subject Faculty Members** (Design & Technology Teacher, English Teacher).
- **1 Campus Facilities Officer / Custodian**.

### 6.2 Structured Usability Protocol
Participants engaged in a 30-minute simulation session comprising three tasks:
- **Task A (Solo Focus):** 10 minutes reading a technical research article in the Solo Desk alcove while simulated hallway background noise played at $65\text{ dB}$.
- **Task B (Team Reconfiguration):** Rearranging four trapezoidal tables from solo configuration into a collaborative pod.
- **Task C (Ergonomics & Clearance Review):** Navigating an accessibility wheelchair / rolling book bag through the central aisle.

### 6.3 Empirical Testing Results

#### Positive Validations
- **Visual Barrier Effectiveness:** 10 out of 10 students reported that the $1200\text{ mm}$ partition prevented eye-contact distraction with passersby.
- **Modularity Speed:** The 4-desk reconfiguration task was completed in an average of **44 seconds**, comfortably surpassing the 60-second target.
- **Faculty Approval:** Teachers commended the open sightlines that allow supervisory monitoring without intruding into student workspaces.

#### Critical Vulnerabilities Identified
- **Noise Bleed between Zones:** Conversational laughter from the 4-person collaborative pod penetrated the solo zone when both operated simultaneously.
- **Elbow Clearance:** Cardboard mockup revealed that desk width ($700\text{ mm}$) felt slightly cramped when a student had both a 15-inch laptop and an open spiral notebook.
- **Power Cord Tripping Risk:** Floor-running extension cables posed a hazard for walking students.

### 7.2 Design Trade-Offs

1. **Acoustic Isolation vs. Space Airflow & Light:**
   - *Tension:* Enclosing solo study desks with full-height walls maximizes acoustic privacy but blocks natural cross-ventilation and sunlight, causing thermal discomfort.
   - *Resolution:* Capped acoustic screen height at $1250\text{ mm}$ (sitting eye-level + 200 mm) with slatted acoustic felt, preserving air circulation while shielding direct line-of-sight acoustics.

2. **Occupancy Density vs. Ergonomic Movement:**
   - *Tension:* Maximizing seating capacity (e.g., 10 seats) satisfies high demand but generates claustrophobic congestion.
   - *Resolution:* Restricted permanent capacity to **7 simultaneous users** (3 Solo, 4 Collaborative), preserving a generous $1000\text{ mm}$ accessibility aisle.

3. **Charging Abundance vs. Fire & Electrical Safety:**
   - *Tension:* Placing multi-plug strips on every table introduces cable clutter and circuit overload risks.
   - *Resolution:* Implemented one certified surge-protected master power bar per module with recessed desktop grommets, strictly prohibiting high-draw appliances (e.g., portable heaters/kettles).

### 7.3 Iterative Prototype Revisions

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Free-standing partition screens | Vulnerable to tipping if bumped | Floor-weighted bracket stabilization with lockable base glides |
| Floor extension cables | Trip hazard and code violation | Overhead suspended conduit drop with modular grommet access |
| Cardboard surface | Moisture absorption and wear | Seal-coated recycled plywood with scratch-resistant matte laminate |

# CHAPTER 8: FINAL SOLUTION: STUDYSPACE SPECIFICATIONS

### 8.1 System Summary

Blending ergonomic modularity, passive acoustic dampening, and clear community behavioral norms, the solution converts underutilized school alcoves into high-productivity academic havens.

### 8.2 Main Features
1. **Focus Solo Alcove (3 Stations):** Individual carrels featuring sound-absorbent acoustic felt side-wings, glare-free warm LED task lighting ($4000\text{ K}$), and integrated book rests.
2. **Collaborative Agile Pod (4-Person Cluster):** Modular desks that assemble into a pinwheel, rectangle, or open horseshoe for peer tutoring and team project production.
3. **Dual-Sided Acoustic Modesty Dividers:** Tackable bulletin felt on the interior (for study notes and sticky memos) and high-density sound dampener on the exterior.
4. **Safety-Certified Integrated Power Raceway:** Master surge-protected strip concealed beneath steel cable troughs, providing 4 grounded AC ports and 4 USB-C PD fast-charging slots.
5. **Community Governance Dashboard:** Aesthetic laser-engraved birch plywood plaque detailing the "Honor Code Study Norms" (Quiet voices, clean desk policy, 90-minute fair-use courtesy guidelines).

### 8.3 Ethical, Social, and Environmental Considerations

#### Ethical Integrity & Equity
- The space is completely non-exclusive and free for all enrolled students regardless of academic standing, track, or organization membership.
- No invasive surveillance cameras or biometric trackers are used; order is maintained through visible, student-owned behavioral norms.

#### Social & Collaborative Well-Being
- Creates an equitable environment for students whose home environments lack dedicated quiet study areas or reliable power.
- Alleviates campus social anxiety by providing clearly defined solo spaces where students can study without fear of unsolicited interruption.

#### Environmental Sustainability
- **Material Lifecycle:** 65% of structural timber is sourced from certified sustainably managed forestry or upcycled local pallet hardwoods.
- **Acoustic Insulation:** Reclaimed polyester fiber fabrics and shredded textile off-cuts replace toxic petroleum-based polystyrene foams.
- **Durability:** Heavy-duty modular joinery allows single-component replacement without discarding entire desk frames.

### 8.4 Universal Accessibility & Ergonomics
- **Wheelchair Clearance:** Desk clearance height measures $720\text{ mm}$ from floor to underside with a minimum width of $850\text{ mm}$, fully compliant with accessibility standards (Batas Pambansa Blg. 344 / ADA regulations).
- **Aisle Dimensioning:** Primary transit paths maintain a clean $1000\text{ mm}$ width, permitting unobstructed passage for wheelchairs, crutches, and custodial carts.
- **Ergonomic Curves:** Tablefront waterfall edges reduce wrist compression neuropathy during prolonged laptop typing.

# CHAPTER 9: EVALUATION AND REFLECTION

### 9.1 What Did We Learn?
The team learned that engineering an effective learning tool or space requires **deep empathetic observation rather than premature technological complexity**. Our initial assumption was that students simply wanted high-tech electronic gadgets or closed pods. Through user testing, we discovered that basic human factors—lumbar comfort, protection from visual glance distractions, and ambient quietness—exert far greater influence over academic concentration.

### 9.2 What Worked Well?
- **Rapid Prototyping:** Moving quickly from 2D sketches to 1:1 cardboard mockups allowed us to detect spatial cramping that was invisible on digital screens.
- **Stakeholder Inclusion:** Consulting both the school custodian and librarian early avoided regulatory violations regarding hallway fire exit widths.
- **Co-Design Sessions:** Inviting students to assemble the modular desks themselves generated immediate buy-in and sense of community ownership.

### 9.3 What Did Not Work (Initial Failures)?
- **Initial Partition Attachment:** The first Velcro-based baffle attachment system deteriorated after 15 reconfigurations, requiring replacement with magnetic steel brackets.
- **Undifferentiated Sound:** Relying solely on wall baffles failed to prevent loud group laughter from carrying; adding soft carpet tile underlays proved essential for impact noise suppression.

### 9.4 How Did We Address the Problem?
The team adopted an iterative, evidence-based mindset. When critical feedback indicated noise bleeding, we did not abandon the group pod; instead, we introduced visual noise indicator cards and placed acoustic dampeners directly between the two zones.

### 9.5 Project Limitations
1. **Sample Size Scope:** Usability testing was conducted with $N = 35$ survey participants and $n = 10$ live testers; wider cross-institutional trials are warranted.
2. **Acoustic Material Constraints:** Professional-grade mass loaded vinyl (MLV) was cost-prohibitive, necessitating rely on upcycled textile composites.
3. **Temporal Evaluation:** Long-term durability and student maintenance behavior over an entire academic school year could not be tracked within the course timeframe.

# CHAPTER 10: CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

### 10.1 Conclusion
The **StudySpace Project** demonstrates the transformative power of Human-Centered Design in resolving everyday educational challenges. By systematically analyzing the cognitive and ergonomic needs of high school learners, the project successfully designed, prototyped, and evaluated a low-cost, modular study corner that balances individual deep focus with dynamic peer collaboration.

Rather than demanding costly building construction, StudySpace proves that existing underutilized campus dead-ends can be repurposed into thriving, dignified learning ecosystems through thoughtful spatial zoning, sustainable acoustic dampening, and empathetic design.

### 10.2 Strategic Recommendations for School Implementation
1. **Institutional Integration:** Present the verified StudySpace layout to the School Governing Council (SGC) for formal adoption in upcoming campus improvement plans.
2. **Phased Campus Deployment:** Pilot one permanent corner in the Senior High School building, monitor usage patterns over one grading period, and refine based on long-term data.
3. **Student Governance Compact:** Empower the Supreme Secondary Learner Government (SSLG) to act as stewards of StudySpace maintenance and conduct campaigns on quiet study ethics.
4. **Cross-Disciplinary Fabrication:** Collaborate with TVL (Technical-Vocational-Livelihood) carpentry and electrical students to manufacture the desk modules on campus, reducing procurement costs while providing real-world student training.
5. **Routine Ergonomic & Accessibility Audits:** Perform biannual inspections of cable raceways, structural fasteners, and wheelchair clearances to uphold safety benchmarks.
